Story Bible: Project Overview & Worldbuilding Guidelines

1. High-Level Premise & Tone
Core Hook: A classic fantasy dungeon crawler set in a low-level dungeon that has experienced an explosive, unnatural surge of cooperative species and aggressive expansion.
The Secret (Late-Game Reveal): Modern humanity accidentally discovered dimensional travel and used this fantasy dungeon as an off-world growth vat/bio-sanctuary—specifically to save Earth’s endangered bee population and accompanying flora/fauna.
Tone: Classic high-fantasy dungeon exploration on the surface, chaotic scavenger front in the middle, and cozy-yet-surreal, grounded sci-fi at the climax.
Narrative Priority: Gameplay-First & Nonlinear. Lore is delivered passively through environmental storytelling, event nodes, party dialogue, and item descriptions. Avoid linear mandatory cutscenes.

2. Structural Narrative Rules
The Ignorant Cast: Characters, adventurers, and monsters within Acts 1 and 2 treat all phenomena as pure high fantasy, ancient magic, or alchemical oddities. No character early in the game recognizes Earth technology for what it actually is.
Modular Character Ensemble: Characters are defined by their faction alignment, tactical philosophy (e.g., Burst vs. Grind), and personal motivation for exploring the dungeon—not by their role in an overarching plotline.
The Gameplay Loop Justification: The dungeon operates on rapid, volatile ecological blooms. Clearing a run triggers a mass spore/pheromone release, re-seeding the dungeon layout and resetting the frontline progress for the next expedition.

No death, no gore. Nothing in the catacombs is a corpse or rots (exception to cute zombie girls). Horror is permitted only as fridge horror — the surface reads silly, warm and horny, and the unsettling part is something the player works out later, on their own, and that no line of text ever states. The Spore Alchemist is the reference: mushroom hat, torn skirt, glowing eyes, drunk and having a wonderful time. She is funny. Contrast that with a blocked idea; The cordyceps-infected zombie. Scary, but more importantly, gross.

3. Act-by-Act Lore Framework

[Act 1: Fungal Depths]   -->   [Act 2: Scavenger Front]   -->   [Act 3: Breach Terrarium]
(Rot, Spores, Mycelium)       (Machinery, Scavengers, War)     (Synthetic, Bees, The Reveal)

Act 1: The Fungal Depths
Theme: Rapid decomposition, cordyceps-style mutations, and expanding mycelial networks.
Visuals: Overgrown stone ruins, flooded aqueducts, bioluminescent grottoes, rot-draped keeps.
Disguised Earth Clues: Perfectly circular fungal rings (petri dish footprints), smooth metallic drainage pipes (industrial plumbing framed as dwarf stonework), flickering cold light sources (dying growth LEDs buried in peat).
Factions/Enemies: Fungal-controlled fauna, moldshapers, cap-armored constructs, infected dungeon scavengers.
Alternate pathways: Fauna-focused areas with plant focuses instead of shrooms

Act 2: The Scavenger Front
Theme: Fantasy cargo cults, opportunistic greed, makeshift war machines, and territorial friction over newly unearthed materials.
Visuals: Wooden scaffolding, overturned mining tracks, makeshift palisades, improvised alchemical labs, and ramshackle war rigs.
Disguised Earth Clues: Low-level dungeon denizens (kobolds, goblins, mercenaries) weaponizing mundane Earth junk without understanding its true origin or purpose.
Factions/Enemies: Kobold mechanics, mercenary vanguards, scrap-armored shock troops, alchemical trappers.

Act 3: The Breach Terrarium
Theme: Dimensional bleed, artificial ecosystems, hyper-cooperative apex species, and full structural exposure.
Visuals: Massive clear acrylic barriers, stark overhead shafts of artificial sunlight, industrial scaffolding wrapped in overgrown hives, clear plastic/glass containers integrated into ancient stone.
The Reveal: Earth's accidental intervention is laid bare. Recognizable terrarium gear, bio-containment labeling, shipping tags, and industrial equipment are fully visible, recontextualizing the entire dungeon.
Factions/Enemies: Hyper-adapted apex bees, automated maintenance constructs, resin-bound guardians, mutated trans-dimensional swarms.

4. The Fantasy Cargo Cult (Act 2 Disguise Framework)
To preserve the late-game reveal, modern Earth equipment in Act 2 must be processed through the lens of primitive fantasy scavengers who treat high-tech scrap as durable raw materials:
ATV / Riding Mower Frames: Flipped over, fitted with wooden platforms, and hitched to cave beasts as heavy war chariots with thick, knobby "leather-stone" wheels.
Car Batteries & Wiring: Strapped to spears or shields with wet leather cords, dipped in saltwater to create sparking "Thunder Boxes" or lightning pikes.
Corrugated Metals & Signage: Cut into crude tower shields or painted over with skulls to serve as heavy scrap armor.
Plastic Hoses & Weed Sprayers: Hooked up to manual bellows and filled with acidic spore-sludge to create alchemical flame/acid-throwers.

5. Content Design & Environmental Storytelling
Event Nodes
Choices should test the player's commitment to their macro-strategy (Burst vs. Grind).
Frame decisions around high-risk immediate gains (e.g., Siphoning volatile battery fluid for temporary burst power) versus long-term setup (e.g., Strengthening scrap shields for persistent defense).
Card & Relic Flavor Text
Act 1–2 Relics: Written from the perspective of fantasy scholars misinterpreting modern items (e.g., a plastic spray bottle named "Vessel of Fine Mist").
Act 3 Relics: Text shifts to literal safety labels, hazard warnings, and field notes from Earth researchers.
Ability Cards: Named after biological, mechanical, or structural concepts (e.g., Spore Cloud, Scrap Barrier, Overcharge, Hex-Shield, Pheromone Marker).
Party Banter
Keep dialogue short (1–3 lines per trigger).
Dialogue focuses on party chemistry, tactical observations, and reactions to the weird, escalating dungeon environment rather than world-ending prophecies.
