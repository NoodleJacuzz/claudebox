Story Bible: the world, the acts, and how lore reaches the player

Rewritten session 42, after the previous version was found to have been overwritten by another AI.
That file is `../Archive/STORY-BIBLE-OVERWRITTEN.md` — not authoritative, kept for quotation.

The Mechanical Bible owns what each act asks of the *player*. This owns what each act is.

# 1. The premise

A low-level dungeon — the sort a first-season party clears for pocket money — has been suddenly and
unnaturally overrun. Something came in from outside, liked it here, and started spreading faster than
anything native ever has. The frontier keeps moving. Nobody above ground agrees on what it is, and
the people going down to find out are not, on the whole, the careful kind.

What it actually is: Earth. Modern humanity stumbled into dimensional travel and used the dungeon as
an off-world growth vat — a sanctuary for a dying bee population and the flora that feeds it. The
equipment is still down there. So are the bees.

# 2. The rule that generates everything

The dungeon has passive magic, and it has always had it. Nothing native minds; they grew up in it.
What is new is that Earth's organisms are soaking in it for the first time, and magic here does not
mutate things at random.

**It makes a thing more of what it already was.**

Fungi are opportunistic, various, and indiscriminate — so they became myconids: many species, mostly
getting along, all of them spreading, none of them in charge. Bees are orderly — so order is what got
turned up. They did not become holy. They became *more bee*, and from outside, maximum bee looks like
a religion.

This is the test for anything new. Ask what the thing is already like, then turn it up until it is a
character. Never ask what would be frightening.

# 3. Tone

*Noodle's, verbatim. This paragraph outranks anything written above or below it.*

No death, no gore. Nothing in the catacombs is a corpse or rots (exception to cute zombie girls).
Horror is permitted only as fridge horror — the surface reads silly, warm and horny, and the
unsettling part is something the player works out later, on their own, and that no line of text ever
states. The Spore Alchemist is the reference: mushroom hat, torn skirt, glowing eyes, drunk and having
a wonderful time. She is funny. Contrast that with a blocked idea; The cordyceps-infected zombie.
Scary, but more importantly, gross.

One addition, in his words on why this is a design rule and not a taste preference:

> the best part about adult games is that just seeing something hot is a reward by itself, it reduces
> player expectations and keeps them forgiving, and what an AI game absolutely needs is the benefit of
> the doubt

An enemy the player is glad to meet is doing half its job before the fight starts. A creature that is
only gross has spent the art budget and paid nothing back.

**Never state the mechanism.** Do not write brainwashing, hypnosis, mind control, thrall, or anyone
being made to do anything. The innocent reading has to stay available: a mushroom hat is a hat. This
is a storefront constraint as much as a tonal one.

# 4. The three acts

Named as in the Mechanical Bible §5, which owns what each act tests mechanically.

    [Act 1: Fungi & Flora]  -->  [Act 2: The Excavation Front]  -->  [Act 3: The Marble Hive]
     the spread itself            the people it happened to         where it came in

### Act 1 — Fungi & Flora

Nearest the surface, where the spread is thickest, youngest and least organised. Two kingdoms took to
the magic and neither is running the place: mushrooms that sprawl and squabble, and flowers that got
ideas. Overgrown stone, bioluminescent grottoes, flooded aqueducts, fungal rings that are a little too
circular.

Factions: myconid society — many species, no single authority, a Matriarch who is more household than
throne. Whatever the spores have already gotten into.

### Act 2 — The Excavation Front

Where the dungeon's own residents are dealing with it: kobolds, goblins, mercenary companies and
opportunists, digging up Earth equipment and fighting each other over it. Wooden scaffolding, overturned
mining tracks, palisades, improvised labs, ramshackle war rigs.

This act owns the cargo cult (§6), and it owns kobolds. Nothing in Act 1 should be carrying scrap
**unless it walked down from here** — an Act 2 native met in Act 1 is a trespasser, and its scrap is
the tell that it does not belong. The Kobold Scavenger and the Drowned Salvager are both read that
way. This is a named exception, not an open door: a creature *of* Act 1 never carries Earth junk.

### Act 3 — The Marble Hive

The far end, where it came in. White stone and gold, terraced comb built like architecture, and
Earth's own flowers grown enormous and strange on dungeon magic. The bees keep it immaculate. They are
courteous, they are warm, they are completely certain, and they do not stop.

Recognisable Earth equipment is fully visible here, integrated into the stone and overgrown, without
comment.

### Sub-acts

**An act is not one map.** The naming, settled session 42:

| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| **Act1-1** | The base mushroom biome every run starts in. It splits into the sub-acts, and all of them lead on to Act 2. |
| **Act1-2** | Catch-all for whichever sub-act follows Act1-1. A term, not a place. |
| **Act1-A / -B / -C** | The sub-acts themselves. Each is a different second half with its own enemies and its own boss — not a difficulty tier and not a region. |

Act 2 is outside demo scope. The same structure is expected to apply there and in Act 3.

#### Act1-A — The Mushroom Frontier *(live)*

> Larger, semi-intelligent myconids have started building a society, using mana-infused mushrooms as
> lumber. Boss is the juggernaut and his little buddy pal friend. Shrooms are notably using more human
> weapons and armor. Mechanically, much less focus on lust.

This is the sub-act already in the game — the Juggernaut is its boss today, and his companion is
already in his reference art. Its enemy slots are currently filled by the taxonomy set (crab, eel,
toad, jelly, kobold), which is what the enemy overhaul recasts as frontier myconids under arms.

The human weapons and armour are the fridge horror. Nobody says where a society with no smiths got
plate and spears from, and nobody asks.

**The Shroud** is the Act1-1 boss whose defeat opens this route (it is not in this sub-act itself; E7
moved it up). It is a colony of black mold that has grown itself into the shape of a robed reaper,
scythe included, and the scythe is a fruiting body rather than a tool it found. It is one organism
doing an impression of one person, and it is what the spread looks like when it gets far enough to
have a shape of its own — which is why beating it is what opens the frontier, where the spread is
thickest.

Recast session 50, replacing a clerk from the Excavation Front. Noodle on that earlier version: *"I
genuinely do not understand what 'He is not drowned — that was the old water theme — only damp,
aggrieved, and still counting' means, it is absolute word salad to my mind, I just felt like the
shadowy reaper dude looked cool, I didn't actually write any of his lore myself, I think it was all
placeholders."*

The clerk used to be the fiction's answer to where the frontier got its plate and spears. Noodle closed
that when the clerk was cut: *"that's not really a big cost, it's a fantasy setting dungeon, kobolds,
adventurers."* The armour needs no named supplier — the dungeon is full of people who had some.

#### Act1-B — Flora

> Flora. Empty, needs filling. Alarunes? Dryads? The AI can generate a neato PvZ style sunflower.
> Mechanically would be the hotspot for venom and poison.

Empty. Alraune and dryad are the starting points; the PvZ sunflower is Noodle's own reference for the
register — bright and silly rather than sinister. This is where Act 1's Venom concentrates.

#### Act1-C — The Pollen Road

> Fey have taken up homes in the dungeons and found the mutated moths here are even more stupid than
> the humans they used to mess with, now they spend all day lounging about, the pollen's started
> affecting their judgement. Mechanically, the most focused on whatever the alternate act 1 lust is.
> Probably charm.

Earth's flowers did not arrive alone — the shipment had pollinators in it. The bees went deep and
became a church. The moths stayed up in the shallow bloom with nothing to organise around, soaked the
magic anyway, and got bigger, furrier and no cleverer whatsoever.

The dungeon's own fey moved in on top of them. Natives grew up in this magic and do not mind it
(§2), so the bloom did not displace the fey — it handed them a species even easier to work than
adventurers, and no reason to ever get up again. They are not running anything. They are lying around
in it, dusted, and their judgement is going.

That is one visual language across two scales — wings, dust, antennae, glow, fur — which is worth
more to the art pipeline than two unrelated rosters would be.

*Do not confuse the fey with Act 2's Old Tenants.* Both are natives; the register is the whole
difference. The Old Tenants lost their corridors to the bloom and are furious about it. The fey were
handed a holiday. If a creature could sit in either, it is written wrong.

**Guardrail:** this sub-act stands closest to Act 3, and turns into a preview instead of a contrast
the moment it gets tidy. No gold, no halos, no hexagons, nothing symmetrical. These are the ones that
never got organised, and the pollen is not helping. The rhyme is the point — the player meets the
disorderly version of the Marble Hive's job long before the hymns start, and is never told.

# 5. The secret, and how it leaks

The Earth origin is a secret, but a poorly kept one on purpose. Noodle:

> Yes, but it's very clearly hinted at. It'd be hard for an observant player to miss if the 100% the
> game and do all the secret content like Anastasia.

**The ignorant cast.** Nobody in Acts 1 and 2 recognises Earth technology for what it is. Everything
is ancient magic, dwarven stonework or alchemical oddity. This is what makes the flavour funny, and it
is why the reveal never needs to be staged — the player assembles it while the cast does not.

**Delivery is passive.** Environmental detail, event nodes, item descriptions, party banter. No
mandatory cutscenes, no lore dumps, nothing that gates on a linear plot.

**Anastasia is the loudest hint and says nothing.** She is from Earth. She went a little crazy down
here, and she treats the fantasy world as a toy she operates through her golems. She does not explain
herself, so nobody around her has any idea — which means every scene she is in reads two ways.

**The Earthling lens (idea, not a commitment).** Noodle:

> Playing a run as an Earthling could introduce a neat spin on the game though, with events and some
> relics changing names.

This is already half-built in the flavour rules below: it is Act 3's naming mode, made available early,
as a character trait rather than a progression unlock.

# 6. The fantasy cargo cult — Act 2's framework

Earth equipment reaches Act 2 through scavengers who treat high-tech scrap as unusually good raw
material. The joke is competence: none of it is used wrong, exactly, just never for what it was for.

- **ATV and riding-mower frames** — flipped, planked over, hitched to cave beasts as war chariots on
  thick knobby "leather-stone" wheels.
- **Car batteries and wiring** — lashed to spears and shields with wet leather, dipped in saltwater for
  sparking "Thunder Boxes" and lightning pikes.
- **Corrugated metal and signage** — cut into tower shields, painted over with skulls.
- **Hoses and weed sprayers** — bellows-fed, loaded with acidic spore-sludge.

# 7. Coming home

**Winning re-seeds it.** Clearing a run triggers a mass spore and pheromone release; the dungeon
re-lays itself and the frontier resets for the next expedition.

**Losing is not dying.** ⏸ *Provisional — Noodle, session 42:*

> Very good question, I don't have an answer, and won't have an answer in time for the demo. For now,
> the adventurers flee, lick their wounds, and treat their growing addictions in the lust events.

So: the party breaks off and gets out. What follows them home is the weakness ledger, and the Lust
Events are where it gets dealt with. This is the loss state's entire fiction for now, and it is
load-bearing for `../lust_events/` — a scene written against it should not assume more than this says.

**Why anyone goes back down.** Each character has their own reason, mostly the adventure or plain
curiosity about the strange new creatures. Nobody is on a quest to save the world, and the dungeon is
not a prophecy.

# 8. Writing rules

**Relic and card flavour.** Acts 1–2 are written by fantasy scholars misreading modern objects — a
plastic spray bottle is a *Vessel of Fine Mist*. Act 3 shifts to the literal: safety labels, hazard
warnings, field notes in someone's real handwriting. That shift is the reveal, and it is the whole of
the reveal.

**Card names** come from biological, structural or mechanical ideas, not from what the card does to a
health bar.

**Party banter** stays to 1–3 lines a trigger, about the party and the room they are standing in.
Chemistry and tactics, never prophecy.

**Event nodes** should test the player's commitment to their macro-strategy rather than their morals:
volatile immediate gain against long-term setup. See the Mechanical Bible.
