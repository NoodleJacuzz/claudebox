RANK 2. She watches futures where she gives in, and gets off on them.

WHAT HAPPENS. She is looking at the futures where she gives in, and touching herself while she
watches them. That is the whole scene. She is not fighting anything and there is nothing to resist.
She is a spectator watching something she finds extremely good, and the fact that it is herself is
part of why she likes it.

HOW IT IS WRITTEN. The on-the-clock voice describes what she is seeing, ornate and measured. The off
duty voice says something very short and very plain at the end. The gap between the two is the joke.

WHY IT IS A RANK 2. Watching futures used to be her job and is now what she does for pleasure. She
knows it and she has not stopped.

IMAGES. Three.
  1. Seated formally, hands still, eyes focused somewhere else.
  2. The same pose, one hand no longer still.
  3. Slumped in the chair, off duty, finished.


HER VOICE, FOR REFERENCE

Cassadora has two voices. On the clock she is the classic fortune teller: ornate, slow, a
little theatrical. Off duty she is blunt and short. Switching between the two is the device every one
of her scenes uses.

She does not give in until rank 3. That is the one hard rule about her. Ranks 1 and 2 she is watching.

The world detail she carries well is what the futures will not show her. Her hints are shaped like
refusals, which is useful, because a refusal explains nothing.
