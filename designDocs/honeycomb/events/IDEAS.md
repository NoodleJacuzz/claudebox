# Lust events — IDEAS

**A menu and a frame.** The frame — the laws, the themes, the empty grids — is what the writing gets
poured into. The menu is the scene material at the back, deliberately over-included so the picking is
done by hand: strike what is wrong, keep what is not.

**None of the scene writing here is Noodle's and none of it is in his voice.** It is a pool of work
already done, to pluck from when it helps and ignore when it does not. Nothing in it is approved.

Where the work is: `EVENTS.md`. The open queue is `EVENTS.md`. The rules a scene must clear and how
a row is entered are `AUTHORING.md`. The arithmetic is `RATE.md`.

**Session 48 rewrote everything but the scene material**, against Noodle's four design goals below and
against the event queue that replaced the old three tables. §§1–8 are new. §§9–19 are the old file:
the measurements still hold and the scenes are still a menu.

**Written plainly, on purpose.** Noodle, on the first draft of §3: *"reading this feels like looking at
abstract art... I'm not sure if the understanding I have of it is because I get what you're saying or
if I've conjured up meaning on my own."* Anything in §§2–8 that needs a second read to parse is a
defect, not a style. §§9–19 have not had that pass yet.

## 1. The four design goals

Noodle's, session 48, verbatim. Everything in this file answers to them.

### Goal 0 — Lore

> Though minor, these lust events (and ingame events in general) are the only way to actually exposit
> information into the game. Every scene should be highly explicit AND/OR lightly explicit + give at
> least some subtle implication to some kind of depth of the world beyond it. These should be extremely
> minor and often just imply details. Example: Nettle venom 1 shows her investigating raw venom. She
> says "What are you? You're not like anyone from around here..." Implying she is knowledgeable about
> plants, and that the fluids of the dungeon's inhabitants are otherworldly.

### Goal 1 — General structure

> event 1 shows the character underestimating the weakness, event 2 shows the character being seriously
> affected by it. Event 3 shows the character willfully sabotaging themselves, unable to control their
> desires, completely dominated by it, or headed into the dungeon alone to get more of what triggers
> their lust (often leading to a lust battle). This is the typical structure, to be bent as needed.
> Genuine character differences are more valuable than the structure itself.

### Goal 2 — In-game events

> The random event nodes should absolutely have ones that trigger based on party members that progress
> their lust, and change depending on that lust. One example: Nettle in the party, A1-1. Nettle falls
> into a tentacle pit, casting a sensory deprivation spell at low venom weakness to avoid the slimy
> sensation. At high venom weakness, she willfully avoids casting it, resulting in higher lust buildup
> from the event or the sacrifice of a random relic to escape.

### Goal 3 — Progress events

> Informal name. Lust events should be checked as a benchmark of progress. A huge boon to the game
> would be the feeling of development as characters progress. Random event triggers could have highly
> conditional opportunities for two characters to interact and show them dealing with the same lust.
> One example: Brienne and Nettle both have high venom weakness, A1-1, Nettle notices Brienne is
> sweating and flushed, and offers to keep watch while Brienne pleasures herself to avoid the
> temptation of throwing herself to some tentacles.

## 2. The rules every scene follows

`AUTHORING.md` already holds the tone rules: no death or gore, never say out loud what the spores
actually do, horror only as something the player works out later, and a scene should feel like a reward
rather than a price. These six come from the design goals above and from session 48's corrections.

### Rule 1 — the hotter the scene, the less it owes the world

Goal 0, corrected by Noodle in session 48:

> Don't forget, the more explicit the scene, the less worldbuilding is required. Each scene should be
> justification, and "it's hot" is justification.

So this is a **budget, not a quota.** The first draft of this document said every scene owes exactly one
world detail. That was wrong, and it would have produced scenes that stop being hot to go and be
informative.

| The scene is | It owes |
|---|---|
| **Explicit** — it gets there, and the images carry it | **Nothing.** It has already justified itself. |
| **Light** — a set-up, a rank 1, a scene that stays clothed | **One small implication**, and only one. |

The template is Noodle's. Nettle, holding a vial, says:

> *"What are you? You're not like anyone from around here..."*

One line of dialogue, doing three things: it's hot, it shows she knows plants well enough to be sure
this isn't one, and it suggests that what comes out of the things down there didn't come from this
world.

When a scene does pay:

- **One detail, and keep it small.** If the player could repeat it back as a fact about the setting, it
  was too much. They should only be able to repeat it back as *something a character said*.
- **Put it in dialogue, never in the narration.** A character notices something odd. Nobody explains
  anything.
- **Never answer it.** The answers live in §18 and no scene is allowed to state one.
- **Pick the detail that character would notice.** That is why it costs nothing — it is already the
  kind of thing she'd say.
- **Never invent world facts to fill the slot.** Session 48's first scene draft had Nettle deduce that
  somebody was *manufacturing* the stuff. Nobody is. It is secreted by plants (§4), and the invention
  would have contradicted the bible in a line the player would remember. **If nothing true is available,
  the scene owes nothing — write it hotter instead.**

### Rule 2 — the three ranks are three different beats

Goal 1, and it is the shape of every arc.

| Rank | Title | What happens | How to tell you got it right |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Sensitised | She doesn't take it seriously. Something happened, she has an explanation, and her explanation is wrong. | She isn't worried about it. The player is. |
| **2** | Susceptible | It's affecting her badly and she knows. She's changed something to cope, and the change is itself a symptom. | You can name the habit she changed and the reason she gives for it. |
| **3** | Undone | She's in on it. She sabotages herself deliberately, or can't stop, or goes back into the dungeon alone for more of whatever does it. That last one is where a Lust Battle belongs. | She knows exactly what it's doing to her, wants it anyway, and goes. |

**Break this where the character is more interesting than the pattern.** Noodle: *"Genuine character
differences are more valuable than the structure itself."* Three characters already break it on purpose
and each break is the best thing about that arc — see Clemence, Cassadora and Anastasia in §3.

### Rule 3 — write what the art can actually make

The scenes and the images are one job, and the image side has hard limits. Noodle, session 48:

> The AI is fantastic at doing what it knows, which is shots of a single character in focus.

> Any extremely specific detail is unlikely to be recognized. Pouring a vial into a beaker? Probably not
> in the cards. Balancing a beachball on the tip of her nose? Forget about it. Throwing a frisbee?
> Maybe, but it's more work than you'd expect. What the AI *is* good at is lewds. Booru tags. on back,
> folded, feet up, hands on own thighs, looking down, spread toes, excessive sweat, put that all in an
> image and every one will have a distinct, measurable influence.

> General concepts are unpredictable. "yoga" will probably give out one of a dozen poses with no
> surefire way to know for sure what we'll get, trying to force it with "downwards dog position" will
> fail.

> This all means that in general, SFW is actually more difficult than NSFW. Because interacting with
> environments, multiple characters, these are hard. They aren't impossible, and duo shots especially
> can be very rewarding, but it's important to know what's an easy win.

What that means at the writing desk:

- **A beat is a pose, not an action.** "She squats over the open sack, looking at the vial" is one
  image. "She decants it into a measuring flask" is not an image at all.
- **One character, in focus.** A two-hander is worth writing when it earns it — §7 is entirely
  two-handers — but it costs, so it should be the point of the scene rather than set dressing.
- **The clothed half of a scene is the expensive half.** A rank 1 that stays dressed and does
  complicated business is the hardest thing in this document to illustrate. If a scene is going to be
  light, keep it *simple* as well as light.
- **Props: glass vials and bottles are reliable.** Noodle: *"usually contained in glass vials or
  bottles since the AI can generate those well."* Anything more specific is a gamble.
- **Write the beats, not the choreography.** A scene of three beats is three images, so a scene should
  fall naturally into two or three poses that each say something different.

### Rule 4 — the image is the page-turn, and the text catches up

Noodle, session 48, on what makes these different from Syrup Town:

> Because of this system, image changes are like page-turns in a comic. The moment the reader turns the
> page, the new image explains what happens next, the text and dialogue clarify.

In Syrup Town an `im` line sits inside a scene that is already flowing. Here the player **presses a
button** and the next picture arrives on its own. So:

- **The image delivers the beat. The dialogue reacts to it.** Do not write text that describes what the
  next picture is about to show.
- **Never telegraph across the turn.** The last line before a page-turn should not set up the image;
  it should make the player want to press the button. The image is the answer.
- **Every beat should move.** Two pictures of the same thing is a wasted turn — if beat 2 is beat 1
  slightly further along, merge them.
- **The gap between beats can carry time, and nothing has to explain it.** Nettle opens a vial on one
  page and is dripping on the next. Nobody narrates the middle.

**The divider and the "Later..." button.** Noodle's own convention, from his Nettle V2 rewrite: a
horizontal divider at the end of a beat means time has passed. The continue button is normally
**"Continue"**; after a divider it reads **"Later..."**. He writes the divider as a bare `t ...` line.
*Not built — see `SCENES-01.md`.*

### Rule 5 — lust events have no choices

Noodle, session 48:

> For now, let's not worry about choice or unlocks for lust events. Any that are finished are better off
> in random pools. Just go ahead and delete them.

So a lust event is a **linear scene**: beats, continue buttons, and the way out at the end. No branches,
no `keepsEventReady` door, no unlocks, no experience bargaining. Everything the old placeholder events
demonstrated about choices belongs in §§6–7's map events instead, where the player is making run
decisions anyway.

This simplifies the queue too: with no choices there is nothing that can leave a row unresolved, so a
row is owed, played, and done.

### Rule 6 — don't write one scene and swap the word

A Venom scene and a Charm scene about the same character are two completely different scenes, and since
session 48 they are not even the same *kind* of thing. §4 gives a test for each.

## 3. What each character does with lust

**Noodle rewrote these in session 48.** His words are quoted; the notes under each are how they were
read. **If a note and a quote disagree, the quote wins.**

Each character has one thing she does with lust, and it stays the same whichever lust type the scene is
about. The lust type changes the circumstances, not her.

### Brienne — Warrior

> No heavy plate, maybe a little too far on the nice genuine person side, she believes strength is the
> most important thing you can have (specifically what you can offer to others, your community, and
> your own self). Not specifically physical strength, but since physical strength is accessible to
> everyone, she believes if you can't do magic or acrobatics you have no real excuse not to be jacked.
> Hence many of her lust scenes will be her shouldering the effects until she cracks an finds the
> nearest outlet for her lust. Self-delusion is what guides her final scenes, with her headed into the
> dungeon unarmed telling herself she'll be strong enough to overcome it.

**Brienne carries it because carrying things is what she's for.** She doesn't dodge it, treat it, or ask
for help. She takes the whole weight and keeps standing, because being the one who can take it is what
she thinks she's offering everyone else. That works right up until it doesn't, and then she goes and
finds the nearest way to get it out of her system, fairly businesslike about it.

- **Her arc:** she shoulders it → she keeps shouldering it → she cracks and finds an outlet.
- **Rank 3 is self-delusion, not surrender.** She walks back down with nothing on her, genuinely
  believing she's strong enough. She isn't lying to anybody. She's lying to herself, sincerely, in the
  same voice she uses for everything else.
- **No heavy plate.** Her art is a white bodysuit, red capelet, one pauldron, one pantsleg. She is not
  armoured up, she is *built* — which is also why she is easy to illustrate, since exercise poses are
  exactly what Rule 3 calls an easy win. **Her shipped in-game description still says "Heavy armour and
  heavier convictions"**, matching neither her art nor this. Flagged, not changed.
- **The world detail she's good at carrying:** gear, and how worn it is. She notices something was made
  by somebody.

### Nettle — Necromancer

> Nettle is absolutely into dissecting things, and since doing it to (living) people is off limits,
> she's an extremely experienced herbalist and necromancer. She has a flaming floating skull companion
> (who doesn't talk, but can be interpreted as judgemental). We can't show handwriting in-game. And
> she's insatiably curious, once she starts wanting to know how something works she can't stop thinking
> about it. She's also very antisocial too, leading to a pretty standoffish attitude and some tension
> with people who don't know her well.

**Nettle takes it apart.** That's what she does with everything. She's a serious herbalist and
necromancer because those are the two fields where taking things apart is allowed. When something new
gets into her, the problem isn't that it's affecting her — it's that she now needs to know *how*, and
she cannot put the question down.

- **Her arc:** she investigates → she can't stop thinking about it → she runs the experiment on herself.
- **She is standoffish.** Short, flat, rude to anyone she doesn't know well. That's the register her
  lines are written in, and it's why her scenes get funnier as she stops managing it.
- **The skull is Jonesy Bones** (named 2026-09-23; `../designBibles/story.md` §10). A flaming floating skull companion. It never talks. It can be read as judging her, and
  that's its whole job in a scene — the one witness she can't be standoffish at.
- **No handwriting.** The game can't show it, so nothing can be carried by her notes.
- **The world detail she's good at carrying:** plants, and what things actually are. **She's the easiest
  character to put a world detail in, which is exactly why she shouldn't carry all of them.**

### Severine — Bloodletter

> Severine is a vampire noble, she doesn't hide it. She's amassed enough wealth she can go galavanting
> and globetrotting as she pleases, she's pretty open about basking in the weaknesses or 'sharing' them
> with her harem as she pleases. She's proud and sadistic. She also flip-flops a lot on the "majesty"
> that comes with being a vampire of her status, her official title is Blood Saint, which is basically
> the same sort of status and responsibility as a Cardinal of the Catholic Church. Most of which she
> ignores because it suits her.

**Severine is having a wonderful time and wants you to know about it.** She is not being worn down by
any of this. She is rich, old, titled, completely open about what she is, and the weaknesses are an
amenity — something to bask in, and something to hand round. She is **proud and sadistic**, which means
the pleasure she takes in her own state is only half of it; the other half is watching what it does to
someone else.

- **This is not a decline arc.** The first draft of this document had her losing composure and being
  the last to notice. Wrong. She notices immediately, approves, and orders more.
- **The flip-flop is the comedy.** *Blood Saint* is a real title with real duties, roughly a Cardinal's.
  She invokes the majesty of it when it justifies something and forgets it exists the rest of the time,
  sometimes inside one scene.
- **The harem is hers and it's on the page.** "Sharing" a weakness is a thing she does on purpose and
  talks about lightly.
- **Her arc:** she samples it → she starts procuring it → she's running it, for herself and for others.
- **The world detail she's good at carrying:** provenance. She can taste where a thing has been, and
  she's travelled enough to have something to compare it to.

### Cassadora — Hexer

> Cassadora is used to averting calamities but she was actually drawn to the dungeon when looking
> through disastrous futures and saw quite a few surrounding herself and the dungeon. She has an
> extremely different air about her when she's "on the clock", being the classic psychic archetype, but
> when she's off duty she's pretty curt. She's extremely curious about the possible futures where she
> succumbs to the lust but only seriously submits to it in the third events. Her second and third are
> usually her watching or masturbating while thinking about them.

**Cassadora is here because she saw this coming and came anyway.** Her job is averting calamities. She
looked through the disastrous futures, found a cluster of them around herself and this dungeon, and
walked in. So she is not a bystander to her own arc — she chose the arc, professionally.

- **Two registers, and switching between them is the device.** *On the clock* she is the classic
  psychic: ornate, measured, a little theatrical. *Off duty* she is curt. A scene that starts in one
  voice and lands in the other tells you everything without a line of explanation.
- **She does not submit until rank 3.** This is the one hard structural note on her. Ranks 1 and 2 she
  is **watching** — and in rank 2 she is watching and touching herself to what she sees. That is the
  scene. She is a spectator at her own future, and that is hotter than a struggle.
- **Her arc:** she sees it and doesn't move → she watches the futures where she gives in, and gets off
  on them → she goes and becomes one.
- **The world detail she's good at carrying:** what the futures won't show her. Her hints are shaped
  like refusals, which is useful, because a refusal explains nothing.

### Cinder — Lancer

> Cinder I feel is the easiest to flanderize, so I'll be careful. An extremely gifted and talented
> adventurer who's never seriously struggled in her life, and what's important is this isn't meant to be
> a rich-person downfall arc. She's actually "That good", and whenever she's not "That good" she works
> like crazy to the point of cracking until she is, specifically because her bravado isn't justified
> unless she feels like she's winning. But again, thrillseeker, extrovert, outgoing, has a "Do you dare
> me to chug this weird juice?" sort of attitude towards the dungeon.

**Cinder is actually that good, and when she isn't she grinds herself into the ground until she is.**
That's the engine. The bravado is load-bearing — it only works if she's winning — so the moment
something beats her, she doesn't sulk or spiral, she *drills*. Which with this particular problem means
dosing herself over and over to build a tolerance she is never going to build.

- **Two things to not do.** She is **not** a spoiled rich girl and this is **not** a downfall. She earns
  everything and she's genuinely brilliant; what gets her is that her fix for losing is more exposure.
- **Her arc:** she chugs it on a dare → it beat her once, so she trains against it obsessively → she
  wins, and winning is the problem.
- **Register:** thrillseeker, extrovert, loud, *"do you dare me to chug this weird juice?"*. Her scenes
  should be the funniest in the set and she is where the reward rule is easiest to honour.
- **The world detail she's good at carrying:** she gets closer to things than anyone. She's the one
  who's actually seen it from too near and describes it wrong.

### Clemence — Confessor

> Clemence believes to sin is to be human, thus a human's right is to be free to sin. To be human is to
> be in God's image, and so sinfulness while still keeping your humanity is the peak of Godliness. She
> easily handles the weaknesses the best out of the cast, the only real issue is she goes way farther
> than anyone else, to the point where she'd bathe in venom aphrodisiacs if she could. Rather than a
> mental breakdown, her broken state when overwhelmed with lust carries the heavy implication she's
> right, and is actually ascending to a higher plane as an angelic divine creature. Also, big yuri
> opportunity as she gently guides the others down the path of debauchery.

**Clemence has a theology and it is working.** She isn't coping, isn't struggling and isn't falling.
She handles all of this better than anyone else in the party, because she has a coherent reason for it:
to sin is to be human, to be human is to be in God's image, and so sinning while staying human is as
close to God as a person gets. Nobody has successfully argued with her.

- **She is the best at it and the furthest gone, and those are the same fact.** The danger isn't that
  she breaks. It's that she has no ceiling — she would bathe in the stuff if a bath were available.
- **Her broken state is not a breakdown.** It is written so the player is left with the distinct
  impression that she was right, and that what is happening is an ascension. Never state it. Never have
  anyone confirm it. It should be the most unsettling thing in the game and no line should admit to it.
- **She guides the others.** Gently, warmly, without pressure, and it works. This is the yuri seam and
  it belongs in §7's two-handers as much as in her own scenes.
- **Her arc breaks rank 1 on purpose** — hers is something she volunteered for, not something that
  happened. Keep the break; it's what makes her arc structurally different rather than tonally.
- **The world detail she's good at carrying:** what the party has been through, since she's been
  absorbing it.
- **Tone watch.** Written as suffering, her arc breaks the reward rule outright. Written as serenity,
  it's the best in the set.

### Anastasia — Chessmaster. *Hidden; `inDevelopment` until she ships.*

> Anastasia is a human, a real human from Earth. Story bible covers it, but she's seriously lost in the
> sauce. All of her lust events are actually her talking about how she got totally hooked on the stuff
> and decided to "Stay here", though she doesn't ever actually explain where "here" is, or where she
> came from.

**Anastasia's scenes are one long unexplained confession.** She is from Earth, she got completely hooked
on what's down here, and she decided to stay. She will tell you all of that, cheerfully, across three
scenes — and never once say where she came from or what "here" means, because she doesn't think it needs
saying.

- **Her arc breaks the three beats too.** She isn't discovering anything; she's been past rank 3 for
  years. Her ranks are how much she tells you.
- **She is the loudest hint in the game and states nothing.** Every line reads two ways. That is the
  whole of her lore function and it must never be closed.
- **Keep her hidden.** Written but never advertised. Nothing that lists or counts scenes may give her
  away — same rule as `shippedCharacterArray()`.

## 4. What each lust type is about

Noodle asked for these listed. A lust type decides where a scene can happen, what it revolves around,
and what getting worse looks like. **Since session 48 the two are not even the same kind of thing** —
venom is a chemical and charm is a change of mind.

### Venom

> it's not capitalized, it's not a specific substance either, it's one of many chemicals secreted by the
> plants at the first layers of the dungeon.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | A thick pink fluid. **Not a named substance** — one of many chemicals the plants in the shallow layers put out. Never write it as *the* venom or as a proper noun. |
| **Where the scene happens** | Anywhere she is. It doesn't need a witness and usually doesn't have one. |
| **What she's holding** | Often a glass vial or bottle — Noodle: *"the AI can generate those well"* — but **not always.** A scene can be nothing but the after-effects, and several should be. |
| **How it gets worse** | She gets hit with it → she builds a routine around it → she goes back down for more. That last step is why venom rank 3 is the natural place for a Lust Battle. |
| **What it does NOT carry** | **Nobody is manufacturing it and nobody is selling it.** Plants secrete it. A scene that implies a supply chain contradicts the bible. |
| **The world detail it can carry** | That it isn't from anywhere normal — Nettle's *"you're not like anyone from around here"*. That is the seam, and it stays open. |
| **How it goes wrong** | A scene where she's simply drugged. She has to be doing something with it — or, if the scene is explicit enough, it doesn't have to justify itself at all (Rule 1). |

### Charm

**Rewritten from scratch, session 48.** The first version of this section had charm as *attention* —
being looked at, noticed, counted. That was wrong.

> Charm is specifically related to the fey, so it's mostly to do with suggestibility and inhibition
> dropping. Brienne goes streaking on a whim, Nettle starts tinkering with Fey magic unaware it's
> affecting her mind and body, their sense of normalcy shifts to become more fey-like, which isn't
> inherently compatible with a sin-free human lifestyle. It is not hypnosis, you must never call it
> hypnosis, even by accident, and if I do, it's a mistake.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | The fey, and what being around them does. **Suggestibility, and inhibitions dropping.** Act 1-C, the Pollen Road. |
| **What it looks like** | She does something she would never normally do — and finds it completely unremarkable. Brienne goes streaking on a whim. Nettle starts playing with fey magic without noticing what it's doing to her. |
| **The actual mechanism** | Her sense of what's *normal* moves, and it moves fey-ward. Fey normal is not compatible with a sin-free human life, and nothing about that is explained to her. |
| **How it gets worse** | One uncharacteristic impulse she doesn't examine → her baseline shifts and she stops noticing the gap → she's running on fey norms, and would have to be told they aren't hers. |
| **Where the scene happens** | Anywhere. It is not about being watched — that was the error. |
| **The world detail it can carry** | That the fey aren't doing anything to her on purpose. They're lounging in it themselves and their judgement is going too. |
| **How it goes wrong** | Writing it as compulsion. **If she's resisting and failing, it's wrong** — she isn't resisting, because it hasn't occurred to her that there's anything to resist. She always wants to. Charm takes away the reason not to. |

**THE HARD RULE.** Noodle: *"It is not hypnosis, you must never call it hypnosis, even by accident, and
if I do, it's a mistake."*

Banned outright, in any scene, any preview text, any result text, any doc: **hypnosis, hypnotic, trance,
mesmerised, entranced, mind control, controlled, thrall, enthralled, spellbound, compelled, made to,
couldn't help it, couldn't stop herself, against her will, puppet, obedient.** This is a storefront
constraint as well as a tonal one, and it is the same rule as `AUTHORING.md`'s *never state the
mechanism* — sharper, because charm is where it is easiest to break by accident.

The safe construction is always: **she did it, she meant it, and she doesn't think it was strange.**

### Penance — Clemence only, and only if it survives the cut

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | A running total. Nothing is done to her — she chooses all of it. The scene is about the number, not where it came from. |
| **How it gets worse** | She starts rounding up → somebody else works out what she's been carrying and says the number → she stops treating it as a cost at all. |
| **Why it's cheap** | Three scenes covers it, and it's the only lust type that goes up without needing the enemies to cooperate. See §9. |

**Measured today** with `../tools/lust-share.js`: venom is on 15 enemy moves, charm 15, restraint 8,
exposure 6, torment 0, penance 0 (Clemence's come from 43 of her own card effects). Restraint and
exposure are still being taught, so §10's untag decision is still open — but it doesn't block §§5–7,
which need only venom and charm.

## 5. THE SCENE LIST — one scene per character, per rank, per lust type

**Every cell below that is still ☐ now has a longer idea entry in `IDEAS-SCENES-01.md`**, written in
session 49: what happens in the scene, why it fits its rank, and how many images it is. This grid stays
the index. That file is the one to write a sketch from.

**42 slots, and this is the list that ships.** Goal 1's three beats across §3's characters. Mark a cell
☐ → ☑ as it gets written. The note in each cell is a starting angle, not the scene.

Columns are the three beats: **doesn't take it seriously · badly affected and knows it · in on it.**
**Cassadora, Clemence and Anastasia break the pattern on purpose** — see §3, and don't straighten them.

### Venom — 21 slots

| | Rank 1 | Rank 2 | Rank 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brienne** | ☑ Took a faceful, says she's fine, works it off outdoors | ☐ Has to deal with it after every descent now, on a schedule | ☐ Walks back down with nothing on her, certain she can out-tough it |

## 6. PARTY EVENTS — map events that read the ledger

**Goal 2**, and Noodle's count: *"In-game events want one per character per lust."* So **14 of them**,
seven per lust type.

These are ordinary `honeycomb.eventArray` entries with a real `weight`, so they turn up on map nodes
during a run. They're gated on the character being in the party, and they change depending on how bad
her weakness already is. They are not Lust Events and the queue never touches them.

**Why they're worth the work.** A Lust Event is the payoff at camp. This is where the ledger actually
costs the player something while they're playing, which is what makes the rank they've been building up
feel real instead of like a number on a sheet.

### How one is put together

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The gate** | The character is in the party, plus the act. Nothing more — an event that also needed a specific rank to appear would almost never come up. |
| **The low version** | Her weakness is below the threshold. She deals with it. The scene is competent, a bit funny, and teaches the player that this hazard exists. |
| **The high version** | Her weakness is at or above the threshold. **She turns down the way out she took last time**, deliberately, and the writing knows she did. |
| **The cost** | The high version costs more than the low one. It isn't a punishment — it's her getting what she wanted and paying for it. |
| **The world fact** | One line, same as always. A hazard is a good place for it: something down there behaves in a way it shouldn't. |

**One event with two branches, not two events.** `choice.condition` decides which choices are offered,
so the low and high versions live in the same entry, and a player who sees both across two runs sees
the same scene change on them.

### What the high version can cost

Noodle, on his tentacle-pit example: *"it could also be a rare card, and gaining weakness is a good
idea too. Though that'd do nothing to a maxxed weakness character."*

| Cost | Can it be built today? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **More weakness** | **Yes** — `{index: "gainWeakness", character, tag, amount}`. | **His catch is right and it matters.** At rank 3 the exposure number keeps going up but the rank doesn't, so a maxed-out character pays nothing. Use it as the cost for ranks 1–2 and swap to something else at rank 3, which `choice.condition` with `weaknessRank atLeast 3` does directly. |
| **Lose a card** | **Yes** — `{index: "removeCardFromDeck"}` with nothing named takes one at random. | "A random **rare** card" specifically would need a rarity filter the effect doesn't have. A plain random card is harsher and works now. |
| **Lose a relic** | **No.** `gainRelic` has no opposite. | One new effect, `loseRelic`. See §8. |

**The cost has to bite at rank 3**, because rank 3 is the whole point of the high version. That rules
out weakness on its own.

## 7. PROGRESS EVENTS — two characters with the same weakness

**Goal 3**, and Noodle has confirmed this is where the character **pairs** belong. His framing is the
design: *"Lust events should be checked as a benchmark of progress... A huge boon to the game would be
the feeling of development as characters progress."*

**These are the reward for the ledger existing at all.** They can't fire for a fresh party, because
they need two characters who have both been through it. So running into one is the game telling the
player how far they've come without putting a number on it. Nothing else in the game says *you've been
playing this a while* out loud.

### What makes them different from §6

§6 is one character alone with a hazard. §7 is two characters **dealing with it together**, and the
tone is the difference. They're conspiratorial, practical and warm. Nobody is rescued and nobody is
caught doing anything. One of them notices, one of them offers, and the offer is matter-of-fact rather
than charged — which is what makes it land.

### How one is put together

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The gate** | Both characters in the party, both at or above the rank, plus the act. That's rare by construction, which is correct — it should feel like finding something. |
| **Who notices** | One of them spots it in the other, **and spots it because she knows the signs from her own experience.** That recognition is the whole beat. |
| **The offer** | Practical, immediate, slightly absurd, freely given. It solves a problem rather than creating one. |
| **The cost** | Usually nothing. This is a reward for progress; charging for it undoes the point. |
| **The world fact** | Often the best of the three sections, because two characters comparing notes can say something neither of them would say alone. |

**Who notices whom.** The one doing the noticing should be the one it's free for: Nettle notices
symptoms, Severine notices appetite, Cassadora notices what's about to happen, Clemence notices what
somebody is carrying. Brienne and Cinder work better as the one being noticed.

**Clemence is the exception worth building on.** Noodle: *"big yuri opportunity as she gently guides
the others down the path of debauchery."* She is the only character who notices AND acts on it, and
she does it warmly and without pressure. Every two-hander she is in should be her doing that.

## 8. What the engine already does, and the one thing missing

Measured this session, so §§6–7 are a build order rather than a wish list.

**Everything §§6–7 need already exists.** A map event supports:

- `event.condition` — checked when the event pool is built (`honeycomb.eligibleEventArray`), so an
  event can be kept out of the pool entirely.
- `{index: "partyContains", character}` — reads the party in the current run.
- `{index: "weaknessRank", character, tag, atLeast}` — **added this session.** In a map event you have
  to **name the character**; `"subject"` means nothing outside a Lust Event and reads as rank 0.
- `{index: "allOf", conditionArray}` — which is how §7's four-part gate gets written.
- `choice.condition` — so one entry carries both the low and the high version.
- `{index: "gainWeakness", character, tag, amount}` — grows the ledger from a map event, through
  `honeycomb.lust.addExposure`, so the per-run rank ceiling still applies.
- `{index: "removeCardFromDeck"}` — with no card named it takes one at random.
- The usual map event vocabulary alongside: `gainResource`, `damageIgnoringTemporary`, `applyStatus`,
  `soothe`, `heal`, `addCardToDeck`, `deckService`, `gainRelic`, `chooseCards`.

**The one thing missing: taking a relic away.** Noodle's PV1 costs a random relic, and there's no
effect that removes one — `gainRelic` has no opposite. By `../BASICS.md`'s rule (*needing engine code
for a new event means a missing verb*) the fix is one `loseRelic` effect, not a special case inside one
event. **Not built** — another agent is in the repo on Anastasia.

**A smaller one:** `{name}` only gets substituted inside a Lust Event, where the overlay knows who the
scene is about. A map event has to write the character's name out. That's fine here, since every event
in §§6–7 is written for one named character anyway.

## 9. Three findings that changed the scope, measured

### The cut can be done by UNTAGGING, not by deleting or retagging

`RATE.md`'s own rule: *"Lust from an untagged source records nothing."* A lust move with no lust tag
still deals Lust, still pushes toward Broken, still reads exactly the same in the fight — it simply
writes nothing to the ledger, so it can never rank up a weakness nobody has written scenes for.

That means B17's cut costs **no fiction at all**. The Cap Brute's Pin does not have to become a venom
move and does not have to stop pinning; it just stops teaching Restraint. Every cut tag keeps its
moves, its flavour and its numbers.

This is the cheapest lever in the workstream and it is worth deciding before anything else, because it
turns "which tags survive" from a content-loss question into a bookkeeping one.

### Penance is Clemence-only, and costs 3 scenes, not 21

`AUTHORING.md`'s open question 3 asks whether Penance collapses into Exposure. Measured:

| | |
|---|---|
| Enemy moves teaching Penance | **0** |
| Clemence card effects teaching Penance | **43**, every one self-inflicted |
| Any other character able to gain it | **none** |

So Penance is not a sixth tag in the ordinary sense. It is one character's private ledger, it cannot
appear on anyone else, and full coverage of it is **3 scenes**. It is also the only tag in the game
whose throughput does not depend on the enemy roster cooperating — Clemence ranks it up by playing her
own cards, every fight she is in.

Against B18's *"it should be conceivable that every run triggers an H-event"*, that makes Penance the
single most reliable scene source available, for 3 scenes of work. **Recommendation: keep it, and do
not collapse it.** Collapsing it into Exposure would throw away the most dependable rank-up in the game
to save nothing.

One detail worth a decision: Clemence's *Take Their Burden* (`clemenceGrant` upgrade) moves Lust off an
ally onto herself **untagged**, so absorbing teaches her ledger nothing. Tagging that transfer
`penance` would make her mechanic and her arc the same thing. Tagging it with the *original* tag would
instead let her inherit whatever hit the ally, which is a different and also interesting character —
but it is a design choice, not a bug, so it is left here rather than changed.

### `RATE.md`'s roster gap is stale, and the gap has closed

`RATE.md` was written session 42 against a 12% lust share. Sessions 44 and 45 rebuilt the roster.
Measured today with `../tools/lust-share.js`:

| | Session 42 | Today |
|---|---|---|
| Enemy moves dealing Lust | 14 of 113 — **12%** | 44 of 157 — **28.0%** |
| Venom | 4 | **15** |
| Charm | 5 | **15** |
| Restraint | 2 | 8 |
| Exposure | 2 | 6 |
| Torment | 0 | **0** |

So B18's stated obstacle — *"the obstacle is roster throughput"* — has largely been fixed by the enemy
overhaul without this workstream touching it. Venom and Charm are now the two heavyweight tags by a
wide margin, which is exactly the shape Act1-B and Act1-C were built to have. **Torment has no source
anywhere in the game** and is a free deletion.

## 11. The spine: two recurring guests, so 42 scenes are one story

The anti-pattern in `AUTHORING.md` is a scene that could be about anyone. The matching risk at 45
scenes is the opposite one — 45 unrelated vignettes that share nothing. A small recurring cast fixes
both at once, because the *character's* reaction to a constant is what makes the scene theirs.

### Venom — the Spore Alchemist

She is already the tone reference (`../designBibles/story.md` §3: mushroom hat, torn skirt, drunk, having a wonderful
time, funny). She turns up at camp between descents because she likes the company and has more than
she can carry. **Nobody ever asks her for anything. She offers.** She is never a dealer, never a
tempter, never sinister, and never explains anything, because she does not think anything needs
explaining.

The arc across ranks is about who is doing the finding:

- **Rank 1** — she offers, once, and it is accepted without much thought.
- **Rank 2** — the character starts looking for her.
- **Rank 3** — no looking is required. She is already there. Or the character has stopped needing her.

Fridge horror, never stated: she is always already at camp. She never remembers being asked. She has
been doing this a long time and has never once run out.

### Charm — the hanger-on

> **Session 48: half of this is still right and half of it is dead.** The fey who followed the party
> home, wants nothing, lounges and is excellent company — that is exactly the register §4 now describes,
> and it is the reason charm reaches the party at all. **The counting is gone.** It belonged to the old
> reading of charm as attention. What this one actually does is simpler and worse: nothing. It is having
> a lovely time and its judgement is going, and being near it moves what the party thinks is normal.

**Noodle likes this one — keep it.** One of the Pollen Road's fey followed the party home, because
that was easier than getting up. It doesn't want anything. It isn't running anything
(`../designBibles/story.md` §4: *"They are lying around in it, dusted, and their judgement is going"*).
It lounges, it comments, and it is extremely good company.

**It is the reason charm reaches the party at all**, and it does the job by existing rather than by
doing anything. Nobody is worked on. It simply lives there being completely relaxed about things a
human would not be relaxed about, and what counts as normal in that room moves a little every week.
Being good company is the whole mechanism.

- ~~**Rank 1** — noticed being noticed.~~
- ~~**Rank 2** — going where the noticing happens.~~
- ~~**Rank 3** — performing, and knowing it.~~
- ~~Fridge horror, never stated: it counts.~~

**Superseded.** The live escalation is §4's: one uncharacteristic impulse she doesn't examine → her
baseline shifts → she is running on fey norms. Fridge horror, never stated: **it doesn't think it is
doing anything, and it is right.**

### Why two guests and not six

Both are cast the player already met downstairs, so no scene has to introduce anyone. Both are
*passive* — they offer and they lounge — which keeps every scene's agency with the party, and keeps
the tone rule (*a scene is a reward, not a toll*) intact by default. Neither ever takes anything.

## 16. The scene pool — camp beats

Scenes that are about the **party**, not one character, and fire on how far the story has come rather
than on anyone's ledger. They are the only ones guaranteed to be seen in order, which makes them where
§2's lore law pays best — a camp beat can deposit something nobody's personal scene could carry.

These are queue rows like any other; the count is the requirement. `["total", 3, "alreadyHere"]` was
the old table's way of saying
`{index: "alreadyHere", character: "brienne", event: "alreadyHere", requirementArray: [{index: "lustEventCount", atLeast: 3}]}`,
and a tag-scoped one adds `tag: "venom"` to the requirement. **Name one character, not `"any"`** — a
fanned-out row is owed to everybody at once, and a beat that happens once in the camp should happen
once. Whoever is named is simply who the scene is staged through.

| Trigger | Working title | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `["total", 1, …]` | **The Ledger** | The first time anyone says out loud that something is being kept track of. Establishes the whole system as a story rather than a stat. |
| `["total", 3, …]` | **Already Here** | The Alchemist is at camp *before* the party gets back. Nobody comments. The first fridge-horror beat, and it is one sentence long. |
| `["venom", 3, …]` | **Same Sentence** | Two characters compare notes and find they have written the same sentence, in different handwriting, about different nights. |
| `["total", 5, …]` | **Resupply** | A crate comes down from the surface with more in it than was ordered, correctly addressed. Somebody up there is paying attention. Ties to the Tallyman and `../designBibles/story.md` §5's leak. |
| `["charm", 3, …]` | **It Counts** | The hanger-on is heard counting. Accurately. It is not asked about. |
| `["total", 8, …]` | **Why We Go Back** | The party stops calling it a side effect. This is the scene that makes the ledger the reason to descend rather than the price of descending — `../designBibles/story.md` §7's open question, answered from the side without committing to a loss state. |

## 17. The scene pool — scenes that work for anyone

Deliberately about *nobody in particular*, which makes them the highest-value scenes per word here:
one row with `character: "any"` is owed once to every character who reaches it, so five of these cover
the whole roster while §5 fills in. Rank-sensitive text is one row per rank.

They are the answer to "what ships if the 42 do not" — and because they are certain rather than rolled,
they are also the cheapest way to make B18's *"it should be conceivable that every run triggers an
H-event"* true on day one.

  getting it mean anything. Cheap, universal, and the only scene in the set where the answer is no.
- **Quartermaster** — somebody reorganised the packs, and there is a compartment nobody admits to
  adding.
- **Watch Rotation** — two members keep volunteering for the same shift, and the rota is in one
  handwriting.
- **The Long Way Round** — the party took a route back that was measurably longer, and everyone had a
  reason.
- **Trading Post** — the Alchemist has more than she can carry again, and is not counting.

Five fallbacks with rank-sensitive text would cover the entire grid at a readable minimum while the
authored spine fills in — which is the answer to "what ships if the 45 do not".
