# Lust events — SCENE IDEAS, round 01

**This is the idea stage for the 36 scenes that have no sketch yet.** Each one is a short description
of what happens in the scene. Nothing here is written as dialogue or narration — that is the next
stage, and it is `SCENES-01.md`'s format.

Each entry has the same four parts:

- **What happens.** The events of the scene, in order, in plain sentences.
- **Why this is a rank 1 / 2 / 3.** How the scene matches the beat it is supposed to be (`IDEAS.md`
  §2, Rule 2). This is the part to disagree with if a scene feels like it is in the wrong slot.
- **The one world detail**, where the scene has one. Rule 1 says an explicit scene owes nothing and a
  light scene owes one small implication, so most rank 1s have this line and most rank 3s do not.
- **Images.** How many, and what each one shows. Rule 3 says the art can only do certain things, so
  an idea that cannot be drawn is not a usable idea. Two images is the normal size. Three is for a
  scene that needs the third.

Everything here comes from the character's section in `IDEAS.md` §3, the lust type in §4, and the
one-line angle already in the §5 grid. Where the grid line was already clear I have just written it
out at more length. Where it needed a decision — usually how many images, or what the last line has
to do — I made one, and that decision is the thing to check.

# VENOM

## Severine — Bloodletter

Her three scenes go: she tries it, she starts collecting it, she is running the whole supply. She is
never losing control of the situation and she never regrets anything.

### SV1 · Vintage — rank 1

**What happens.** Severine gets a faceful of venom in a fight. She likes it. Within about a minute she
has stopped thinking about the fight and started thinking about which members of her harem should try
some. She is enjoying herself and she is already planning the next time.

**Why this is a rank 1.** She is not worried, and the player should be. She has just had a strong
reaction to a substance she cannot identify, and her only response is that she wants more of it. She
never asks what it is or where it came from.

**The one world detail.** She tries to place the taste, the way she would place a wine, and cannot.
She has travelled everywhere and it does not match anywhere. She says this once and then drops it.

**Images.** Two. (1) Chest up, licking it off her own fingers, eyes closed, enjoying it. (2) Sprawled
backwards, flushed, counting names off on her fingers.

### SV2 · Amenity — rank 2

**What happens.** She has had the stuff collected in quantity and she is handing it round. The scene
is her describing, in detail and with obvious expertise, what it is doing to the people she has given
it to — while it is very plainly doing the same thing to her. She talks the whole way through. She
justifies the expense by saying that a Blood Saint has a duty of hospitality, and by the end of the
scene she has completely forgotten she has a title.

**Why this is a rank 2.** The habit she changed is that she now keeps a supply of it, and the reason
she gives for that is hospitality. Both halves of Rule 2's test are answerable: the habit is the
supply, the excuse is the hospitality.

**Images.** Three. (1) Reclining with a bottle, a partner kept `offscreen`, completely composed.
(2) Same couch, her composure going, still talking. (3) Her on her own in frame, no longer talking.

### SV3 · The Cellar — rank 3

**What happens.** She is running the supply now — hers, her harem's, and anyone else she decides
deserves some. The thing she will not accept is being the least affected person in her own house, so
she doses herself to keep up with what she is handing out. She has stopped counting how much she takes.

**Why this is a rank 3.** She knows exactly what it is doing to her and she wants it anyway. The
difference from the other rank 3s is that she does not lose an argument with herself — she never has
the argument. She decided this a long time ago and is enjoying the result.

**The last line.** She says she has stopped counting, and makes it clear she stopped because counting
was something you do while a thing is still a treat.

**Images.** Three. (1) Standing over a rack of bottles, choosing. (2) Drinking, shot from below,
unhurried. (3) On the floor among the bottles, delighted.

## Cassadora — Hexer

Her three scenes go: she sees it coming and does not move, she watches futures of herself giving in and
gets off on it, she goes and becomes one of them. She does not actually give in until rank 3 — that is
§3's one hard rule about her.

She has two voices. On the clock she is the classic fortune teller: ornate, slow, a bit theatrical.
Off duty she is blunt and short. Switching between the two is the trick every one of her scenes uses.

### CV1 · Foreseen — rank 1

**What happens.** She sees the splash coming about four seconds before it happens, and stands there
and lets it hit her. Afterwards somebody asks why she did not move. She is off duty by then, so the
answer is short and unhelpful: it happened in most of the futures, so there was not much point.

**Why this is a rank 1.** She is not worried and the player is. She has already decided this is going
to happen to her repeatedly and she finds being asked about it tedious.

**The one world detail.** She can see what happens next but not what the stuff actually is. She says
so once, flatly, and does not explain further. The value of the line is that it refuses to answer
anything.

**Images.** Two. (1) Standing in it, on the clock, ornate and unbothered, eyes closed. (2) Close up,
off duty, flat expression, wiping her face.

### CV2 · Audience — rank 2

**What happens.** She is looking at the futures where she gives in, and touching herself while she
watches them. That is the whole scene. She is not fighting anything and there is nothing to resist —
she is a spectator watching something she finds extremely good, and the fact that it is herself is
part of why she likes it.

**How it is written.** The on-the-clock voice describes what she is seeing, ornate and measured. The
off-duty voice says something very short and very plain at the end. The gap between the two is the
joke.

**Why this is a rank 2.** The change is that watching futures used to be her job and is now what she
does for pleasure. She knows it and she has not stopped.

**Images.** Three. (1) Seated formally, hands still, eyes focused somewhere else. (2) The same pose,
one hand no longer still. (3) Slumped in the chair, off duty, finished.

### CV3 · Attendance — rank 3

**What happens.** She goes back down alone and becomes the version of herself she has been watching.
Before she goes she checks one last time, sees herself decide to go, and then stops checking.

**Why this is a rank 3.** It is the same shape as your Nettle rank 3 — she talks herself through it,
gives up, and acts — except the person she loses the argument to is a version of herself she already
watched lose it. She knows precisely what she is walking into.

**Lust Battle.** Venom rank 3 is the natural place for one (`IDEAS.md` §4). She stays dressed the
whole time, so she can have one where Brienne cannot.

**Images.** Three. (1) Walking back down alone, from behind, cloak on. (2) Arrived, looking at
something kept `offscreen`. (3) Her face, close, the on-the-clock voice gone.

## Cinder — Lancer

Her three scenes go: she drinks it on a dare, it beats her once so she starts training against it, she
finally out-lasts a dose and that turns out to be the problem. She is genuinely good at everything and
this is not a downfall story. These should be the funniest scenes in the set.

### CiV1 · Dare — rank 1

**What happens.** Somebody dares her to drink it and she does it without breaking stride. The scene is
the twenty minutes afterwards, in which she insists she is fine, is obviously not fine, and announces
that she is going to go and lie down in a completely normal way — and then describes exactly how
normal it is going to be, at length.

**Why this is a rank 1.** She is not worried at all. She won the dare, which as far as she is concerned
is the only part that was ever in question.

**Images.** Two. (1) Mid-swig, bottle up, grinning, thumbs up. (2) Sitting on the floor against a wall,
legs out, sweating, still grinning.

### CiV2 · Tolerance — rank 2

**What happens.** It beat her once, so she is training against it. Doses on a timer, reps counted,
arousal treated as a number she can bring down with practice. She keeps very good records. The records
show the number going up every single time, and she does not draw the obvious conclusion. The scene
never points this out.

**Why this is a rank 2.** The habit is the training schedule. The reason she gives is that she lost
once and does not intend to lose again. She is proud of the schedule.

**Images.** Three. (1) Sitting cross-legged with bottles lined up, businesslike. (2) Mid-set,
trembling, counting out loud. (3) Flat on her back, arm over her eyes, calling it a warm-up.

### CiV3 · Personal Best — rank 3

**What happens.** She out-lasts a dose that should have put her on the floor. She wins. Then, because
she can take that much now, the next one has to be bigger, so she reaches for it while she is still
shaking.

**Why this is a rank 3.** She knows what it does to her, she wants it anyway, and she goes and gets
more. Her version of your Nettle rank 3 shape has her talk herself into it out of delight rather than
resignation — the thing she says before she acts is that this is a new record.

**The last line.** Short, flat, and about the next dose rather than this one.

**Images.** Three. (1) On her knees, arms up, victorious and wrecked. (2) Reaching for another bottle,
still shaking. (3) Her face, close, laughing, eyes gone.

## Clemence — Confessor

Her three scenes go: she takes a dose on purpose that was not aimed at her, she stops limiting herself,
she finds somewhere she can get right into it. She is calm all the way through. She is not suffering
and she is not falling apart, and her rank 1 deliberately breaks the pattern because hers is something
she volunteered for rather than something that happened to her.

### ClV1 · Volunteered — rank 1, and it breaks the pattern on purpose

**What happens.** A spray is about to land on a teammate. Clemence steps across and takes it herself,
before anyone has registered that there was anything to take. She is calm and efficient about it. The
unsettling part is that nobody else in the scene notices that anything happened at all.

**Why this breaks rank 1.** Every other rank 1 is something that happens to the character. Hers is
something she chose, which is the structural difference §3 asks for. She still matches the important
half of the rank: she is not worried, and the player should be.

**The one world detail.** She knows exactly who the dose would have landed on and exactly how badly it
would have gone, because she has been keeping track of what each of them is carrying since the first
descent.

**Images.** Two. (1) Stepping across with her arm out, calm, taking it across the front. (2) Afterwards,
hands folded, dripping, completely composed.

### ClV2 · Unrationed — rank 2

**What happens.** She had been portioning it out and treating that as a discipline. She has stopped.
The scene is her explaining why the limit was the wrong thing to have, in the same voice she would use
to say grace, and the explanation is coherent and difficult to argue with.

**Why this is a rank 2.** The habit she changed is that there is no longer a limit. The reason she
gives is theological and she believes it completely.

**Tone warning.** If this reads as her losing control it is wrong. She is calmer here than in her rank
1.

**Images.** Three. (1) Seated, a bottle beside her, mid-sentence, lit warmly. (2) The bottle empty, the
sentence not finished. (3) Her, still explaining.

### ClV3 · Immersion — rank 3

**What happens.** She would get right into the stuff if there were enough of it, so she goes and finds
somewhere there is. She undresses without hurrying, gets in, and is completely at peace.

**Why this is a rank 3.** She knows what it does and she wants all of it. What makes it different from
the other rank 3s is that nothing about it is a loss of control — she is not undone, she has arrived.

**The rule that matters most here.** The player should come away with the feeling that she was right
about everything and that something is happening to her that is more than just the venom. **No line
says this and nobody in the scene confirms it.** The last image does all of it.

**Images.** Three. (1) Standing at the edge of it, undressing, unhurried. (2) In it, head back, arms
out. (3) Her face, lit, peaceful. The third image is the one carrying the implication.

## Anastasia — Chessmaster · *hidden, `inDevelopment` until she ships*

Her ranks are not a progression. She has been past rank 3 for years. What changes between her three
scenes is how much she tells you. Every line should be answerable two ways and no scene closes either
reading.

### AV1 · Habit — rank 1

**What happens.** She mentions in passing that she got hooked on the stuff, the way someone mentions
they used to smoke. She does not treat it as a confession and nothing in the scene treats it as one
either. The player is supposed to notice that she said something enormous and that she did not notice
she said it.

**Images.** Two. (1) Relaxed, seated, talking, holding a bottle she is not looking at. (2) Closer, same
ease, mid-anecdote.

### AV2 · Stayed — rank 2

**What happens.** She mentions that at some point she decided to stay. She never says where "here" is,
because to her it does not need saying, and nothing in the scene asks her.

**Images.** Two. (1) Leaning back, looking up at nothing in particular. (2) Her face, close, fond.

### AV3 · The Whole Thing — rank 3

**What happens.** She tells the entire story from the beginning, cheerfully and at length, and explains
none of it. Every word that would settle where she came from or what happened to her is one she does
not supply, and she does not appear to be hiding anything.

**Images.** Three. (1) Mid-story, animated, hands moving. (2) Mid-story, further gone, still talking.
(3) Afterwards, quiet, pleased with herself.

# CHARM

**The test for every scene here is the one in `IDEAS.md` §4: she did it, she meant it, and she does
not think it was strange.** If a scene has her resisting and failing, it is the wrong scene. She is not
resisting, because it has not occurred to her that there is anything to resist. §4 also has the list of
words that are banned outright in any charm writing.

## Brienne — Warrior

### BC1 · Airflow — rank 1. **Your own example. Write this one first**

**What happens.** She takes her clothes off and goes running through the grass, because she felt like
it. When somebody asks, she explains that it is about airflow, and the explanation is actually a good
one — managing heat is something she has thought about professionally. She is entirely sincere and
slightly evangelical about it. She is the most comfortable person in the scene.

**Why this is a rank 1.** It is one impulse she does not examine. She is not embarrassed and does not
think anything unusual has happened.

**Images.** Two. (1) Mid-run through grass, from behind, delighted. (2) Stopped, hands on hips, facing
front, explaining her reasoning.

### BC2 · Kit List — rank 2

**What happens.** She has stopped putting things back on after taking them off, and she has a specific
practical reason for each piece she is no longer wearing. The scene is her going through her kit and
giving the reason for each one, completely straight. Every reason on its own is sensible. There are
six of them.

**Why this is a rank 2.** The habit is that things stay off now. The reasons are the symptom — she has
one ready for each piece, which means she has thought about each piece and concluded the same thing
every time.

**Images.** Two. (1) Seated with her gear laid out, sorting through it, half dressed, businesslike.
(2) Standing, bag over one shoulder, what is left on her not covering much, ready to go.

### BC3 · Dressed for the Road — rank 3

**What happens.** She is not undressed. She is dressed properly, by fey standards rather than human
ones, for walking the Pollen Road rather than for a dungeon. It suits her, she knows it suits her, and
she is pleased with how she looks. Nobody in the scene is in a position to tell her that the standard
she is dressing to is not hers.

**Why this is a rank 3.** She is running on fey norms completely and the gap has closed. She would have
to be told, and there is nobody to tell her.

**The one world detail.** She notices that what she has on was made by somebody who clearly knew what
they were doing. She does not wonder who.

**Images.** Three. (1) Full body, front, wearing it, pleased. (2) Walking, from the side, relaxed.
(3) Close, looking down at herself, approving.

## Nettle — Necromancer

### NC1 · Field Test — rank 1. **Your own example. Write this one first**

**What happens.** She starts poking at fey magic because she cannot leave a mechanism alone once she
has noticed it. It starts affecting her almost immediately and she does not notice, because she is
busy. Her curiosity is how it gets in — nothing has to be done to her, she opened the door herself and
is taking notes on the other side of it.

**Why this is a rank 1.** She is not worried, because she has not noticed. The player can see it
happening over her shoulder.

**Images.** Two. (1) Crouched over a small fey working, absorbed, unaware. (2) The same crouch, further
along, still absorbed, very obviously affected.

### NC2 · It Works — rank 2

**What happens.** The spell works. So does the other thing it is doing to her, and she notices that as
a result — an effect worth writing down, in exactly the same flat voice as the first result. She files
both under findings. She is now repeating the working, and the reason she gives is that a result has
to be replicated.

**Why this is a rank 2.** She knows it is affecting her. She has decided that is interesting rather
than alarming, and she has built a habit around continuing.

**Images.** Three. (1) Casting it, green light, pleased with herself. (2) The second result arriving,
mid-sentence. (3) Her sitting back, describing her own condition in the third person.

### NC3 · Peer Review — rank 3

**What happens.** She is better at fey magic than the fey are, and about as bothered by what it has
done to her as they are, which is not at all. Her usual standoffishness has quietly turned into
something more like fey aloofness and it fits her far too well. The skull is in the scene. It does not
say anything, and it does not have to.

**Why this is a rank 3.** She is running on their norms and has no reason left to stop.

**Images.** Three. (1) Standing among fey lights, at ease, unimpressed by them. (2) Mid-working, the
green and the fey colours mixed. (3) Close, half-lidded, the skull in frame.

## Severine — Bloodletter

### SC1 · Charmed — rank 1

**What happens.** She meets the fey, finds them delightful, and agrees to something on the spot. She
does not read the terms, because agreeing to things is the boring formality that happens before the
interesting part. She would not have read them on a normal day either, which is what makes this hers
rather than anyone's.

**Why this is a rank 1.** One agreement, not examined, and she is pleased with how the evening went.

**Images.** Two. (1) Seated, gesturing at a fey kept `offscreen`, charmed and charming. (2) Afterwards,
pleased, not yet aware that anything has changed.

### SC2 · Terms — rank 2

**What happens.** She keeps agreeing to things. The scene is her defending several of the agreements
at length and with precedent, and being just as certain about them as she used to be about the
opposite. Her sense of what is beneath a noble has moved and she has not noticed it move.

**Why this is a rank 2.** The habit is that she now says yes as a matter of course. The reason she
gives is that a noble honours what she agrees to, which is true and is not the point.

**Images.** Three. (1) Mid-agreement, expansive, one hand out. (2) Honouring one of them, partner
`offscreen`, composed. (3) Her face, close, satisfied.

### SC3 · Court — rank 3

**What happens.** She is holding court by fey rules and she considers them an improvement on her own.
She cites the office of Blood Saint to justify fey protocol, which is the most ridiculous thing she has
ever done, and she does it with total conviction.

**Why this is a rank 3.** She has adopted the whole system, and she is arguing for it rather than
excusing it.

**Images.** Three. (1) Seated as though enthroned, the court arranged around her, kept `offscreen`.
(2) Mid-ruling, imperious, half dressed. (3) Close, delighted with herself.

## Cassadora — Hexer

### CaC1 · Four Seconds — rank 1

**What happens.** She sees herself agree to something four seconds before she does it, watches it
happen, and agrees. Afterwards she is off duty and short about it: she knew, that was the point, and
she does not want to discuss it.

**Why this is a rank 1.** One impulse she did not examine, and she is not worried about it.

**The one world detail.** She can see herself say yes. She cannot see what she is saying yes to, and
she says so once, flatly. She has never been able to see terms and has never explained why.

**Images.** Two. (1) Standing, on the clock, eyes white, mid-vision. (2) Off duty, shrugging, already
agreed.

### CaC2 · Different Futures — rank 2

**What happens.** She is watching futures and touching herself to them again, the same as her venom
rank 2, but what she is watching has changed. These are not disasters any more. She has not asked
herself when the catalogue changed.

**Why this is a rank 2.** She knows what she is doing and she is doing it deliberately. The thing she
has not examined is what she is choosing to look at.

**Images.** Three. (1) Seated formally, on the clock, hands still. (2) Hands no longer still, voice
still ornate. (3) Off duty, slumped, flat.

### CaC3 · Unchecked — rank 3

**What happens.** She stops checking, and finds out what she has agreed to the same way everybody else
does. For a fortune teller to choose to go in blind is the most reckless thing anyone in the party has
done, and she is completely relaxed about it.

**Why this is a rank 3.** She has given up the one advantage she has, on purpose, because it was
getting in the way of enjoying herself.

**The last line.** Short and off duty. She does not know what happens next and she says so as though it
is good news.

**Images.** Three. (1) Eyes open and ordinary, the white gone. (2) Mid-something she did not see
coming, surprised and enjoying it. (3) Close, laughing, unguarded.

## Cinder — Lancer

### CiC1 · Worst Possible Pairing — rank 1

**What happens.** A fey offers her a dare. She has never turned one down, and the fey have never met a
limit, and the scene is those two facts meeting for the first time. She wins it, and then goes past
what the dare actually asked for, because winning by a margin is better.

**Why this is a rank 1.** One dare, taken without thinking about it, and she is delighted with herself
afterwards.

**Images.** Two. (1) Squaring up, grinning, fey kept `offscreen`. (2) Mid-dare, committed, already past
what was asked.

### CiC2 · Escalation — rank 2

**What happens.** The dares are getting worse and she believes she is the one making them worse. She is
not. The fey keep accepting, which to her reads as winning, and nothing in the scene tells her
otherwise.

**Why this is a rank 2.** The habit is that she proposes now instead of accepting. The reason she gives
is that she is ahead.

**Images.** Three. (1) Mid-dare, arms wide, loud. (2) The next one, fewer clothes, louder. (3) Her
face, close, thrilled.

### CiC3 · Undefeated — rank 3

**What happens.** She wins a dare she had no business winning and cannot stop laughing about it. The
laughing goes on well past the point where it is triumph, and nobody stops it, including her.

**Why this is a rank 3.** She has won so far past her own limits that the winning is no longer under
her control either.

**Images.** Three. (1) The moment she wins, arms up, wrecked. (2) Still laughing, on the floor.
(3) Close, laughing, tears, delighted.

## Clemence — Confessor

### ClC1 · Theologically Interesting — rank 1

**What happens.** She finds the fey theologically interesting and says so at length. The debate is the
scene and she enjoys it enormously. She wins about half of it, and concedes one small point that she
should not have conceded and does not think is important.

**Why this is a rank 1.** One thing given away without examining it, and she is pleased with how the
conversation went.

**Images.** Two. (1) Mid-debate, seated, animated, fey kept `offscreen`. (2) Afterwards, pleased, one
thing about her different.

### ClC2 · The Case — rank 2

**What happens.** She has started making the argument to the rest of the party, gently, one person at a
time, and she is good at it. She applies no pressure anywhere in the scene. That is exactly why it
works. **This is where the yuri thread starts.**

**Why this is a rank 2.** The change is that she is no longer only doing this to herself. The reason she
gives is that she would be keeping something good to herself otherwise.

**Images.** Two. (1) Sitting close to a teammate, talking, warm — the teammate `offscreen`, or a proper
two-character shot if the budget is there. (2) The teammate's answer, and Clemence's face.

### ClC3 · Consensus — rank 3

**What happens.** Half the party has come round to her position. Every one of them did it freely and
nobody was pushed, which is the part that should unsettle the player rather than reassure them. She did
not convince anybody of anything they did not already half believe.

**Why this is a rank 3.** Her version of rank 3 is not losing control, it is the argument being over
and her having won it.

**The rule from her venom rank 3 applies here too.** The implication does all the work and no line
confirms it.

**Images.** Three. (1) The group, arranged comfortably, nobody uneasy. (2) Her among them, being agreed
with. (3) Her face, close, at peace.
